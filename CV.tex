\section{Research}

My research follows a compiler-driven approach to agile hardware-software co-design. Three coupled threads form a closed loop: architecture description and exploration discovers and formalizes reusable hardware capabilities; chip DSL and compilation express those capabilities and lower them into implementations; compiler optimization and LLM systems improve the resulting compiler, execution, and co-design workflows. My current work creates an extensible benchmark of kernel programming and optimization tasks for dataflow-architecture chips, develops a layered knowledge and skill system across multiple compilation paths, and builds full-spectrum data collection and training workflows for continual model evolution. The ultimate goal is a methodology and workflow that enable models and agents to rapidly acquire expert-level kernel optimization capabilities for new chip architectures.

\ResearchStoryDiagram

\smallskip
\noindent \ResearchParagraphTitle{Architecture description and exploration.} I combine compiler analysis, formal methods, and architecture DSLs to represent and search design spaces while connecting customized hardware capabilities to programmable software. \textit{ISAMORE} (\ProjectLink{https://doi.org/10.1145/3779212.3790162}{DOI}) discovers reusable custom instructions by anti-unifying equivalent program fragments; \textit{Cayman} (\ProjectLink{https://doi.org/10.1109/DAC63849.2025.11132875}{DOI}) identifies accelerator opportunities in complete applications while co-optimizing control flow and data access; \textit{APS} (\ProjectLink{https://doi.org/10.1109/ICCAD66269.2025.11240817}{DOI}, \ProjectLink{https://github.com/pku-liang/APS}{GitHub}) and \textit{Aquas} (\ProjectLink{https://uv-xiao.github.io/assets/pdf/aquas_iccad.pdf}{ICCAD 2026}) describe customized architectures through DSLs and expose them through complete hardware/software compiler stacks.

\smallskip
\noindent \ResearchParagraphTitle{Chip DSL and compilation.} I create DSLs and intermediate representations at multiple abstraction levels, then develop compilation and synthesis passes that optimize timing, microarchitecture, implementation selection, mapping, and scheduling. \textit{Hector} (\ProjectLink{https://doi.org/10.1145/3508352.3549370}{DOI}, \ProjectLink{https://github.com/pku-liang/Hector}{GitHub}) provides multi-level MLIR foundations; \textit{Cement} (\ProjectLink{https://doi.org/10.1145/3626202.3637561}{DOI}) and \textit{Cement2} (\ProjectLink{https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15073}{preprint}) introduce cycle-deterministic DSL and temporal-transaction abstractions; \textit{Clay} (\ProjectLink{https://doi.org/10.1109/ICCAD66269.2025.11240669}{DOI}) makes ASIP instructions microarchitecture-aware; \textit{SkyEgg} (\ProjectLink{https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.15323}{preprint}) jointly optimizes implementation selection and scheduling with e-graphs.

\smallskip
\noindent \ResearchParagraphTitle{Compiler optimization and LLM systems.} I explore LLMs for compiler and hardware-software co-design workflows, together with compiler and system support for LLM workloads. \textit{EggMind} (\ProjectLink{https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17364}{preprint}) synthesizes equality-saturation strategies; \textit{IntelliC} (\ProjectLink{https://github.com/uv-xiao/IntelliC}{GitHub}) studies inspectable representations for human-model compilation; \textit{Spine} (\ProjectLink{https://github.com/arch-of-shadow/spine}{GitHub}) organizes verification-bounded cross-layer co-synthesis; \textit{PTO Runtime} (\ProjectLink{https://github.com/hw-native-sys/simpler}{GitHub}) supports dynamic kernel fusion and distributed execution of compiled task graphs on Ascend chips and LingQu SuperPods; \textit{Hive} (\ProjectLink{https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.17353}{preprint}) provides multi-agent inference infrastructure.
